faq:
  - question: "How does the AI-based contaminant detection work?"
    answer: "BioBlick uses high-resolution cameras and computer vision. The AI first detects the pile of bio-waste and then identifies contaminants on its surface. From this it calculates a surface contaminant share – an objective figure for assessing the delivery."
  - question: "Is the surface contaminant share related to the share by mass?"
    answer: "Yes. Joint evaluations with INFA found that the share by surface and the share by mass correlate. The camera analysis therefore provides an automated, digital approximation for a practical assessment of delivery quality."
  - question: "How does BioBlick help pass costs on to those responsible?"
    answer: "Objectively documenting contaminated deliveries creates solid evidence towards suppliers or municipal partners. When heavily contaminated loads have to be rejected, re-sorted or treated separately, cost responsibility is much easier to justify."
  - question: "What is the surface contaminant share?"
    answer: "It describes the share of visible contaminants on the surface of a delivery – that is, how much of the visible pile surface is covered by foreign matter. This makes deliveries objectively comparable and highlights conspicuous suppliers or routes."
  - question: "Which contaminants does the AI detect?"
    answer: "Typical non-biogenic contaminants, in particular plastics, films, packaging and other visible foreign matter. Which classes are detected in detail depends on the training, the camera angle and your plant’s requirements."
  - question: "How does BioBlick help with documentation duties?"
    answer: "Every delivery is documented with image data, detected pile area, detected contaminant area and calculated contaminant share – a basis for internal quality control, conversations with suppliers and evidence for authorities or clients."
  - question: "Can the contaminant share appear on the weighbridge ticket or delivery note?"
    answer: "Yes. BioBlick can be connected to existing weighbridge systems, delivery notes or ERP systems, so the contaminant share is linked directly to each delivery and built into your intake process."
